Dyslexia:

What is Dyslexia?
Dyslexia is a neuro biological learning difference that affects how the brain processes language.
Dyslexia is a specific learning difficulty that mainly affects reading and language-related skills. A person with dyslexia may have difficulty with reading words accurately or fluently, spelling, or connecting written letters with their sounds.
A common myth is that dyslexia is an eye problem where letters float or flip backwards (or) that it reflects a lack of intelligence. In reality dyslexia does not mean a person has low intelligence. A person can have normal or high intelligence and still have dyslexia.



Common difficulties

A person with dyslexia may experience:

🔤 Difficulty recognizing or decoding unfamiliar words
📖 Reading more slowly or with less fluency
✍️ Frequent spelling difficulties
🔄 Confusing similar-looking or similar-sounding words/letters
🧩 Difficulty breaking words into individual sounds
📝 Difficulty remembering how certain words are spelled
⏱️ Needing more time to read and process written material

The exact difficulties may vary from person to person.


Causes / Factors Associated with Dyslexia

🧬 Genetic factors — Dyslexia can run in families and has a hereditary component.
🧠 Differences in brain development — Brain networks involved in reading and language may develop and communicate differently.
🔤 Phonological processing difficulties — Difficulty in identifying and manipulating the individual sounds in spoken words.
📚 Reading and language processing differences — The brain may process written language less efficiently, making decoding and fluent reading more difficult.

why is age group important in predicting whether a person has dyslexia or not?

Age is important because reading ability and eye-movement behavior naturally change as people grow. If we don't account for age, a machine-learning model might mistake normal age-related differences for signs of dyslexia.

why it is affected mostly or seen mostly on 5-12 years age group?

Some important terms we need to know:
Fixation: A period when the eyes remain relatively still and focused on a particular word or part of the text to process the visual information.


Saccade:
A rapid movement of the eyes from one location to another, allowing the reader to move from one word or part of the text to the next.


Regression:
A backward movement of the eyes to a previously viewd text, usually occurs when the reader needs to recheck or process information again.
